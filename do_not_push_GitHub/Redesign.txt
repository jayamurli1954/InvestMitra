Short answer:

The mistake would be forcing users to think too much.

Let’s break this down calmly.

---

# 🧠 Why Holding-Level Segmentation Is Actually Better

If you segment at portfolio level:

Core Portfolio
Tactical Portfolio

Then what happens when:

* You hold TCS long-term (core)
* But you also take a short-term tactical position in TCS later?

You’re stuck.

Holding-level classification gives flexibility.

It matches real hybrid behavior.

---

# ⚖ Is It Too Heavy For Users?

It becomes heavy only if:

You force them to classify everything manually with thinking effort.

It becomes light if:

You give a smart default and minimal friction.

---

# 🏗 How To Make It Lightweight (Correct UX)

When adding a holding:

Dropdown:

Portfolio Type
(Default = Core)

Options:

* Core (Long Term)
* Tactical (Medium Term)
* Swing (Short Term)

Most users won’t even change it.

Advanced users will.

That’s perfect.

---

# 🧘 Even Better UX Option (Cleaner)

Instead of:

Core / Tactical / Swing

Use softer labels:

* Long Term
* Medium Term
* Short Term

Internally map:

Long Term → core
Medium Term → tactical
Short Term → swing

Feels less intimidating.

---

# 🎯 Why It’s Not Heavy

You’re asking users only ONE additional question:

“What is your intention for this holding?”

That is actually healthy investing behavior.

It improves discipline.

---

# 🧠 Real Psychological Benefit

When market falls:

If it’s tagged Long Term:
You don’t panic.

If it’s tagged Tactical:
You review thesis.

If it’s tagged Short Term:
You accept volatility.

Segmentation reduces emotional confusion.
